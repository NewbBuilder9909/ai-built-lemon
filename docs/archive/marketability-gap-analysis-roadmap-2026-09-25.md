# Marketability gap analysis and roadmap

Review date: 25 September 2026. This document consolidates the current
commercial position from the repository's product, security and commercial
evidence. It is a positioning and roadmap aid, not customer validation.

## Recommended market position now

The strongest current offer is **not** a broad self-service SaaS platform.
It is an **assisted weekly delivery evidence diagnostic** for services firms
whose delivery, time, resource or contract reporting is assembled across
multiple systems and manual reconciliation.

The near-term promise is narrow:

- review one recurring delivery or programme pack;
- expose missing evidence, stale data, unresolved ownership and unsupported
  totals;
- return an exception-led review with an owner and a next decision.

The safe public message remains a **controlled pilot and assisted diagnostic**,
not universal connector-led onboarding or an open-market subscription claim.

## Strengths to sell

### C-suite and delivery leadership

- Delivery, staffing, contract and cost evidence can be discussed in one
  review flow.
- The product shows uncertainty directly: stale publication state, unresolved
  identities, missing estimates and incomplete coverage are visible rather
  than hidden behind clean totals.
- The presentable management questions are concrete: which commitments need
  action, where delivery pressure exceeds the evidence, and which exceptions
  need an owner before the next review.

### PMO and reporting owners

- This is the clearest current buyer and champion.
- The existing product already supports blocked/open/overdue work, planned
  allocation separated from delivery completion, freshness and audit signals,
  estimate coverage and tenant-aware controls.
- That creates a credible alternative to a weekly pack that looks complete
  while hiding reconciliation gaps.

### Enterprise buying committees

- The codebase shows stronger-than-prototype operating discipline:
  documented release gates, tenancy, audit controls, migration plans,
  security hardening and connector maturity boundaries.
- That matters to a CTO, security approver or procurement reviewer even when
  the initial commercial offer is services-led.

## Gaps limiting broader sale today

### Positioning gap

The repository spans delivery reporting, staff operations, contracts,
branding, skills/evidence, service analytics and executive review features.
That breadth is useful as future product surface but weakens the opening
story by blurring the first buyer, first pain and first measurable outcome.

### Proof gap

There is not yet enough customer evidence for:

- willingness to pay;
- repeat usage;
- measured preparation-time reduction across repeated cycles;
- improved decision quality;
- production deployment confidence at broader rollout scale.

### Buying-journey gap

The application supports assisted pilots, not frictionless software purchase.
Open gaps remain around self-service onboarding, automated provisioning,
billing, entitlement reconciliation and customer-facing deployment proof for
an unrestricted SaaS claim.

### Operational and product gap

Prospects with technical due diligence will still see open work around
durable background execution, deployment hardening, shared-hosting proof and
connector maturity beyond controlled validation.

## Buyer-by-buyer summary

| Buyer | What resonates now | Current limitation |
|---|---|---|
| CEO / COO / delivery sponsor | Which commitments need action now, and where is delivery pressure masked by reporting noise? | Outcome improvement at scale is still a hypothesis; keep the story on decision-ready exceptions, not business transformation claims. |
| CFO / commercial lead | Cost coverage, unpriced effort, contract visibility and currency-aware review. | Position as qualified financial evidence, not a forecasting or margin-automation product. Attribution and acceptance still need customer proof. |
| CTO / security approver | Tenancy direction, auditability, operational controls and documented deployment boundaries. | Needs stronger deployment and connector acceptance evidence before a broad platform claim. |
| PMO / reporting owner | Reconciled weekly pack, visible gaps, freshness honesty and cross-tool review. | Needs easier repeat onboarding, a stronger quality ledger and dated scenario/capacity support. |

## What to sell now, and what not to lead with

### Sell now

- **Weekly Delivery Evidence Diagnostic**
- **Assisted reporting reconciliation**
- **Controlled pilot for services firms with multi-tool reporting pain**

### Do not lead with yet

- broad all-in-one operations-suite positioning;
- self-service SaaS onboarding;
- universal mapping or connector breadth;
- strong ROI, savings or perimeter-isolation claims;
- mature multi-market enterprise-platform language.

## Best initial market

Prioritise services consultancies or agencies that:

- run a weekly portfolio or delivery review;
- combine data from at least two systems;
- have a named PMO, delivery owner or operations lead who already spends
  material time reconciling the pack;
- can provide a sanitised representative export for one repeatable decision.

This is a better qualification signal than generic product interest or a long
feature wish list.

## Commercial roadmap

### Phase 1 — sharpen the proposition (30–60 days)

Focus all external messaging on one use case: a reconciled weekly delivery
review. Reposition the wider modules as supporting foundations or future
expansion, not the opening offer. Standardise the public page, outreach copy,
diagnostic proposal and evidence pack around the same problem statement,
buyer, boundaries and proof language.

### Phase 2 — close controlled-pilot gaps (60–120 days)

Complete the most important controlled-pilot gates: stronger durable sync and
recovery evidence, clearer source-connection ownership, improved deployment
assurance, repeatable tenant/admin/source setup workflow, and better PMO-
friendly quality and reconciliation views.

### Phase 3 — prove commercial value (3–6 months)

Run paid diagnostics with real buyers. Measure baseline and assisted
preparation effort, accepted findings, repeat-purchase intent, support/setup
effort and persona-specific objections. Use that evidence to refine pricing,
scope, sales qualification and the next delivery priorities.

### Phase 4 — productise the repeatable workflow (6–12 months)

Automate only the repeated, measured bottlenecks from paid engagements.
Package deployment, security and onboarding evidence for enterprise review
before expanding connector breadth or attempting a wider self-service motion.

## Strategic priorities

1. Narrow the message.
2. Prove one recurring PMO/delivery use case.
3. Win 2–3 paid assisted engagements.
4. Measure repeatability and buyer value.
5. Automate only what repeats.
6. Expand connector breadth and suite narrative later.

## Plain-language recommendation

Do not market ProgrammePulse as a broad all-in-one business operations
platform yet. Market it first as an **assisted delivery evidence and
reporting diagnostic** aimed at **PMO, delivery and COO-led buyers**, with
C-suite value framed as **better decisions from more trustworthy weekly
evidence**.
